# Methodology and findings: Atlas of Human Mobility

Generated by `uv run python -m analytics.exploration.eda` from the pipeline outputs. Every number below is computed, none typed by hand. Honesty tags: OBSERVED (counted from source), DERIVED (computed from observed), PREDICTED (model output for data we have), SIMULATED (scenario output).

## 1. Scope and data

For-hire trips in New York City, 2024: yellow taxi, green taxi and high-volume FHV (Uber, Lyft, Via, Juno). No subway, bus, bike or private car, so "demand" means for-hire trip demand. Location granularity is the TLC taxi zone (263 areas); there are no coordinates and no trajectories. Timestamps are naive America/New_York wall clock. Weather is one station (Central Park, GHCN USW00094728) applied citywide. Sources and licence terms: `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`.

### 3.5 Missingness, leakage and bias notes

- **Platform-reported HVFHV fields.** `trip_miles` and `trip_time` come from the platforms, which TLC says it cannot verify. Speeds are derived from them, so speed is a proxy, not measured road speed. Trips that fail validation are dropped, which could bias speeds if failures correlate with congestion (not testable here).
- **Airport effect.** Airport-zone trips (1, 132, 138) have a trip-weighted median speed of 21.6 mph versus 11.7 mph citywide; they are long, highway trips and inflate speed in a few zones. Clustering and BPR are fit at zone and borough level, where this mixing is partly absorbed; it is not removed.
- **Speed is conditioned on trip distance mix.** A zone's median speed mixes short slow trips and long fast ones; midday and overnight differences partly reflect trip length, not only traffic.
- **DST.** Naive wall-clock timestamps cannot separate the repeated 01:00 hour on 2024-11-03; that Sunday-01:00 hour-of-week mean is inflated by about 1/52 (1.9%). The 02:00 Sunday cell has 52 occurrences, not 53; denominators are derived from the data (distinct dates with trips), so the missing hour is handled.
- **Survivorship and selection.** Only completed, recorded trips exist. Demand that was never served, or trips that took other modes, are invisible.
- **Single weather station.** Central Park rain is applied to every zone.
- **Ecological fallacy.** Zone-level patterns say nothing about individuals; only aggregates are published.

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Time bucketing and zone dimension

Hour = hour of the pickup timestamp; day of week Monday = 0; hour-of-week = dow * 24 + hour. Hour-of-week means divide annual counts by the number of distinct dates on which that (dow, hour) occurs. Zone polygons come from the TLC shapefile (EPSG:2263), reprojected to WGS84 with pyproj, simplified with a 8e-05 degree tolerance (Douglas-Peucker, topology preserving), coordinates rounded to 5 decimals; the shapefile has one record per LocationID 1..263 (no duplicate IDs to dissolve; the code dissolves any that appear). `zones.geojson` is 378,936 bytes. Transform correctness is tested against JFK (132), Times Square (230) and Central Park (43).

### 4.2 Hour bands

| id | label | hours |
|---|---|---|
| overnight | Overnight 12-6 AM | 0-5 |
| am_peak | AM peak 6-10 AM | 6-9 |
| midday | Midday 10 AM-4 PM | 10-15 |
| pm_peak | PM peak 4-8 PM | 16-19 |
| evening | Evening 8 PM-12 AM | 20-23 |

### 4.3 Hour-of-week profiles, peak hours, corridors

Profiles are zone x hour-of-week pickup means per service and for all services. Peak hours are argmax of citywide mean trips: weekday search windows 05-11 (AM) and 15-21 (PM); weekend Sat+Sun mean. Corridors are directed OD pairs with origin != destination and known zones; per band the top 300 (and top 2000 for all hours) with >= 11 trips are published with exact (not approximate) p10/p50/p90 duration and speed.

### 4.4 Congestion indicator

For each zone, free-flow speed = the 85th percentile of the zone's hour-of-week median speeds over 02:00-05:59 (up to 28 cells, each needing >= 30 trips; zone needs >= 14 valid cells), all services pooled. `speed_ratio` = hour-of-week median speed / that free-flow speed (below 1 means slower than the zone's own overnight speed). Borough free flow uses the same rule on borough-level medians. Why p85 and not the median: the median of overnight speeds includes late-night noise and slow bar-closing traffic; p85 is a standard free-flow proxy. Robustness to this choice is in 5.5. It is a proxy built from for-hire trips, not a measured road speed.

Borough AM-peak (weekday 06-09) speed as a share of free flow: Bronx 56.1% (11.3 of 20.1 mph), Brooklyn 61.3% (10.2 of 16.6 mph), Manhattan 47.6% (10.2 of 21.3 mph), Queens 63.1% (12.8 of 20.2 mph), Staten Island 72.2% (15.4 of 21.3 mph).

### 4.5 Network

Weighted directed graph of known-zone OD pairs with origin != destination and >= 11 trips (59,313 edges). PageRank: damping 0.85, weight = trips. In/out strength = sum of trips. Betweenness: shortest paths with edge length = 1/trips, so heavy flows are short links and paths prefer well-travelled corridors; computed exactly (263 nodes, no sampling). The highest-betweenness (hinge) zone is LaGuardia Airport. Interpretation caution: betweenness here measures position in the flow-weighted graph, not physical routing, and airports score high because they connect to almost every zone with large flows.

Features: each zone's 168-hour mean pickup profile (all services), L1-normalized so only the shape of the week matters. Zones with fewer than 5000 annual pickups are excluded (7 zones, including those with no pickups; the rest are listed in `clusters.json`). K-means (n_init 10, fixed seed), k in 3..8 chosen by silhouette:

### 4.7 Weather association

### 4.8 BPR scenario model (SIMULATED output, OBSERVED fit data)

For a borough b and hour-of-week h: v = mean trips per hour (all services, pickup in the borough); TTI = free_flow_speed / median_speed (equivalently t / t0). BPR: TTI = 1 + alpha * (v / C)^beta. alpha and beta are fitted by bounded nonlinear least squares (scipy `least_squares`, alpha in [0, 50], beta in [0.2, 10], six starts). **Identifiability:** alpha, beta and C cannot all be recovered from (v, TTI) pairs, because alpha * C^-beta is the only combination that matters. We therefore fix C := the borough's p95 hourly volume (a normalization, not an engineered road capacity; `capacity_index`, trips/hour), and alpha is identified. Scenario functions (pure, in `analytics/bpr.py`, mirrored by `bpr_test_vectors.json`):

- `scenario_speed(obs, v, C, alpha, beta, dm, cm) = obs * (1 + alpha (v/C)^beta) / (1 + alpha (v dm / (C cm))^beta)` : anchored to the observed speed; exactly `obs` at dm = cm = 1 (tested).
- `scenario_fitted_speed(ff, v, C, alpha, beta, dm, cm) = ff / (1 + alpha (v dm / (C cm))^beta)` : the model curve; equals the fitted baseline at dm = cm = 1 (tested).

**Reading the fit honestly.** Demand and speed both follow the clock, so v/C is partly a proxy for time of day; the curve describes how pace co-varies with volume across the week, which is not proof that adding demand would slow traffic by the same amount. Fitted beta is near 1 in most boroughs (classic BPR uses 4), so the curve is close to linear in v/C and extrapolation beyond max v/C (above) is not supported by data; the site must flag it. R^2 values are moderate (borough level 0.50 to 0.79, zone-pooled train 0.16 to 0.46): volume explains a meaningful but incomplete share of pace variation. The holdout metrics track the in-sample ones, so there is no overfitting signal, but the model is cross-sectional in time-of-week, not a causal traffic model.

## 5. Validation

### 5.1 Reconciliation
Raw rows equal kept plus exclusions per service-month; published zone_month, od_matrix_totals and daily totals reconcile to kept rows after unknown-zone and suppressed counts (`tests/test_reconciliation.py`). A slow test re-counts raw and interim Parquet.

### 5.2 Baselines
BPR is compared with (a) a constant free-flow baseline (TTI = 1) and (b) a constant equal to the training mean TTI; it must beat both on the Oct-Dec holdout (asserted in `tests/test_models.py`). Clustering is compared with randomly permuted labels (4.6).

### 5.3 Temporal holdout
See 4.8: fit on Jan-Sep, scored on Oct-Dec, with free-flow speed and C also taken from the training period.

### 5.4 Reproducibility
All randomness is seeded; artifacts contain no wall-clock timestamps (`generated_at` is the latest raw retrieval time). Tests hash two runs of k-means and of the BPR fit and require equality; the whole artifact set is rebuilt from raw by `uv run python -m pipelines.run_all`.

### 5.5 Robustness to the free-flow definition
Borough beta (R^2) when free flow is defined differently:

| median_0205 | 1.28 (0.64) | 0.93 (0.48) | 0.98 (0.59) | 2.57 (0.54) | 1.32 (0.78) |

R^2 is stable across definitions, but beta moves (for example the median definition raises beta), so beta should be read as definition-dependent. This is the main sensitivity of the scenario model and is shown to the user as a limitation.

## 6. Small-cell suppression and ethics

Cells with 1 to 10 trips are suppressed (null) in every published count; zero stays zero. The headline suppressed figure is the 25,018 trips in OD pairs with fewer than 11 annual trips withheld from `od_matrix_totals.json`. Across all artifacts 100,516 cells were suppressed (artifacts overlap, so per-artifact figures in `reconciliation.json` must not be added). Only aggregates are published; no trajectories or individuals.

## 7. Failure cases and limitations

- The BPR relation is correlational and nearly linear; scenarios far outside observed v/C are extrapolation.
- Clustering finds a coarse typology (k = 3 by silhouette); finer neighborhood types are not supported by weekly-shape alone.
- Speed is a for-hire proxy; slow zones with few trips have noisy medians and are masked below 30 trips.
- Weather: one station, daily resolution, association only.
- Betweenness favours airports and the Manhattan core.
- Zones with no or few pickups (including EWR) are excluded from clustering and BPR.
- The first-pass contract named five exclusion reasons; `null_timestamp` was added, and bad distance includes missing or NaN values.
